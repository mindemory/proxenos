# Data sources

Every source is optional and works on its own. Start with one or two and add more later, in any order. Each ingester writes monthly files into your vault, and re-running one is always safe: records merge by id, so nothing is duplicated.

## How data gets in

- **Downloads.** Request an export from the service, put the zip (or the unzipped folder) in the repo's `data/` folder or in `<vault>/inbox/`. In the desktop app, choose **Sync now** from the menu-bar or tray icon, or wait for the next sync; from a terminal, run `proxenos import`. It recognizes each export, unpacks it, hands it to the right ingester and files the download away. Exports that arrive in several zips (Google Takeout, Meta) are imported together.
- **Live sources.** WhatsApp (its local store), Gmail (IMAP), Microsoft 365 and Outlook (Microsoft Graph), Google Calendar (its API), Apple Mail (its local store), Slack, Strava, Bluesky and public mentions are read directly, and `proxenos sync` refreshes them. `sync` also imports anything waiting in the inboxes, so one command keeps everything current. The page's Connect view shows every source and whether it's connected, with buttons to sign in to Gmail, Microsoft 365, Google Calendar, Google Docs and Slack, to read WhatsApp now, and a box for Beeper's token. [The guide](guide.md#install-the-desktop-app) walks through each in the desktop app.
- **Folders you name.** A Spotify export, a website repo and slackdump runs live wherever you keep them. Point Proxenos at them in the page's Settings or in `.env` (see [the settings below](#folder-settings)).

The `ingest` commands in the tables are for reading one source by hand. Anything that arrives as a download can go through `proxenos import` instead. Settings live in the page's Settings (saved to `~/Library/Application Support/Proxenos/settings.json`) and keys and tokens in the macOS Keychain; running from source, `.env` works too and is read first. [.env.example](../.env.example) lists them all.

### Health records

Your medical records themselves, in FHIR, the standard format providers' systems share them in. Each record becomes one plain record under `raw/healthrecords/`, by kind: lab results and vitals (`observations`: name, value, unit, reference range and the record's own flag, such as high), `conditions`, `medications` (prescriptions and medication statements), `encounters` (visits), `reports` (diagnostic reports: what they report and their conclusion), `allergies`, `immunizations`, `procedures` and `documents` (title and date only; the document itself isn't kept). Other kinds (the patient, practitioners, claims, coverage and so on) are counted and left out.

| Source | What it adds | How to get it | Command |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Apple Health records** | The records of every provider connected in the Health app's Health Records | On the iPhone: Health, your picture, Health Records to connect a provider; then Health, your picture, Export All Health Data. Put `export.zip` in `data/` or `<vault>/inbox/` and run `proxenos import`: it reads the zip's `clinical-records` folder, one record per file, and nothing else in the export (steps, workouts and the rest). Apple names every export `export.zip`, so rename a newer one (say `export-2026-11.zip`) before dropping it in, or the import takes it for the one it already unpacked. | `proxenos ingest healthrecords --export PATH` (the zip, or the folder it unzips to) |
| **Patient portal records** | The same, from a portal or app that lets you download your record as FHIR R4 JSON: a Bundle (`.json`) or NDJSON files | Put the files in a folder (or zip them) in `data/` or `<vault>/inbox/` and run `proxenos import`; a folder of FHIR files is recognized when nothing else claims it. A loose `.json` file isn't picked up on its own: use the command. Many portals, MyChart among them, download a visit summary as C-CDA (XML) or PDF instead, which this reader doesn't parse (a PDF is read as a note). | `proxenos ingest healthrecords --export PATH` (a file, a folder or a zip) |

What stays out: no one is named in these records. The patient, practitioners and authors are dropped, and only organizations and places stay. The observers that build the self-model don't read them, since your clinicians wrote them, not you. The care clone does (see [Privacy](#privacy)).

Records from the same provider read twice, from Apple Health and from a download, or from two exports, are merged by their FHIR id. DSTU2 records, which some older Apple Health connections hold, are read where their fields match R4's.

The tests' records are written by hand in the shape of the FHIR output of [Synthea](https://github.com/synthetichealth/synthea), the open-source synthetic patient generator (Apache-2.0), which made it possible to build and test this reader without anyone's real records. No Synthea file is copied into the repo.

Services with no API for personal messages (Facebook, Instagram, LinkedIn, X, Spotify's full history) only update when you request a fresh export. Do it every few months and import it; what overlaps is a no-op.

Missing a source? [CONTRIBUTING.md](../CONTRIBUTING.md) shows how to add one.

### A manuscript in Google Docs

Not a source: nothing is read from it on a sync. It's how a project's manuscript goes to Google Docs, for you to edit, comment on and share with co-authors there, and comes back to git.

1. In the Google Cloud project that holds `GOOGLE_CLIENT_ID` (Gmail's and Google Calendar's), turn on the **Google Drive API** (APIs & Services, Library).
2. Press **Sign in with Google** on the Connect page's Google Docs card, or run `proxenos auth gdocs NAME ADDRESS`. It's a sign-in of its own that asks only for `drive.file`: Proxenos can see and change only the files it makes itself, never the rest of your Drive. It saves `GDOCS_<NAME>=address:oauth:<refresh token>` with your other secrets.
3. On Projects & papers, open a project's **Manuscript** and press **Open in Google Docs**. The manuscript at its branch's tip goes up as a Google Doc in that account's Drive (Markdown as Markdown, LaTeX as plain text), private to you until you share it from Docs, and opens in a new tab. **Bring back its changes** puts what changed in the doc on a branch of its own, a step for you to accept or discard, with only the paragraphs changed in Docs changed. Its comments show beside it, and are never sent to a model.

`proxenos project gdocs ID [open|fresh|pull|comments]` does the same from a terminal.

### Your projects in a cloud drive

Not a source either: nothing is read from it on a sync. It keeps each project as a folder in a cloud drive you already sync on this computer, for you to read, edit, comment on and share in Word or Google Docs, and brings what you change in a Markdown manuscript back to git.

1. Install pandoc, which makes the Word files: `brew install pandoc` on a Mac, else [pandoc.org/installing](https://pandoc.org/installing.html). `PROXENOS_PANDOC` says where it is when it isn't on the PATH. Install tectonic too, which makes the PDFs: `brew install tectonic`, else [tectonic-typesetting.github.io](https://tectonic-typesetting.github.io/). `PROXENOS_TECTONIC` says where it is. Its first build fetches the TeX files it needs, so it wants the network once. For a Markdown paper, pandoc can use XeLaTeX, LuaLaTeX, pdfLaTeX or Typst instead, when one is installed and tectonic isn't.
2. Pick a folder your drive syncs and set it in Settings, under Projects, or as `PROXENOS_PROJECT_FOLDER`. On a Mac, Google Drive for desktop's is under `~/Library/CloudStorage/GoogleDrive-<you>/My Drive/`, Dropbox's and OneDrive's are under `~/Library/CloudStorage/` too, and iCloud Drive's is `~/Library/Mobile Documents/com~apple~CloudDocs/`. It has to be there already: Proxenos doesn't make it, so a drive that isn't running gets nothing.
3. Each night, after the projects and their steps (or `proxenos clone folders` by hand), every project gets `<folder>/<group>/<name>/`, under `Other` when it has no group, its names made safe for Windows too. A paused project gets one only when it has something to show.

| File | What it is |
|---|---|
| `<name> - manuscript.docx` | The manuscript at its branch's tip, read from git, never your checkout, with the figures beside it |
| `<name> - manuscript.pdf` | The same version, made in the same checkout so the two always match: LaTeX typeset by tectonic as it is, with its own bibliography; Markdown by pandoc. Only when nothing on this computer makes PDFs does the PDF committed beside the manuscript come instead, and `About this folder` says so |
| `<name> - response to reviewers.docx` and `.pdf` | A response to reviewers or a rebuttal in the manuscript's own folder, found by its name (`RESPONSE.md`, `rebuttal.tex`), from the same version, so co-authors read the letter with the paper; and any other file the project lists under `"documents"` in its entry, each by its own name. They go one way: change them in git |
| `Supplement/` | The tables, figures and data files the manuscript names, copied from its repository: by name or path (`results/S1_table.csv`), or by a label the file's name carries, whatever its case and separators (`Table S4` or `Supplementary Table 4` finds `S02_tableS4_note_types.csv` or `table_s4.csv`, `Fig. S2` finds `FigS2_calls.png`; a table's label finds only `.csv`, `.tsv` and `.xlsx` files, a figure's only images). Not the figures the manuscript shows itself (Markdown's `![...](path)`, LaTeX's `\includegraphics`): its copies have those. Each is copied under the label it goes by, with its own extension, never its repository name: `Table S4.csv`, `Figure S2.png` (from the caption's label, else the one the file's name carries), and `Supplementary Data 1.json`, `2` and on for a file the manuscript names without a table's or a figure's label, numbered in the order it first names them (a `Supplementary Data 3` of its own keeps its number). A copy under an older name goes, while it's as Proxenos wrote it |
| `Supplementary information.docx` and `.pdf` | When a table or a figure is left: a title, then each table (`.csv`, `.tsv`, and `.xlsx` when openpyxl is installed) and each figure under the caption the manuscript gives it, in the order it names them, then the data files, each by its label with what the manuscript's own list of supplementary materials says of it, or its label alone. It names nothing by file, path or extension, as a paper's supplement doesn't. A table longer than 40 rows or wider than 12 columns is cut there, with a line saying the full one is, say, Table S4, beside the document. With only data files left there's none, and one made before goes while it's as Proxenos wrote it |
| `Where it stands.docx` | The project's status, as the night wrote it |
| `What's known, and the question.docx` | Its brief |
| `Drafts.docx` | Its drafts still open, each with its title |
| `About this project.docx` | What `projects.json` says of it: what it's about, its people, your notes, whether it's paused |
| `About this folder.docx` | When the manuscript was made, from which branch and commit, what its PDF is, and what comes back |

**What comes back.** What you change in a Markdown manuscript, in Word or in Google Docs (editing the `.docx` itself: a copy saved as a Google Doc isn't read), comes back the next night as a step on a branch of its own, from the commit the copy was made from, for you to accept or discard on the Projects view. It's converted back to Markdown and merged as the Google Doc's changes are: only the paragraphs you changed change, and the rest keeps its own Markdown to the byte. Tracked changes count as accepted. Comments come back too, on Markdown and LaTeX alike: each night the new ones, with the passage each is on and who left it, are queued as a step that makes them in the manuscript; resolved ones are left out. Anyone the file is shared with can comment, so only your own comments (left under your name, `PROXENOS_NAME`) run as they come; anyone else's wait as a step of their own until you approve it on the Projects view. While that step waits, newer changes wait in the copy. A LaTeX manuscript is made with its citations and references written out from the `.bib` files beside it, so its edits go one way: change the `.tex` itself, or leave comments, which come back as above. The supplement and the PDFs never come back, and neither do the notes.

**The PDFs and the supplement.** The manuscript's PDF is made from the same commit, in the same checkout, as its Word copy, and both are made again together, only when the manuscript or the files beside it change: a LaTeX build takes tens of seconds, so a night where nothing changed builds nothing. One that fails (a missing package, say) leaves what was there, says why in `About this folder`, and isn't tried again until the paper changes. The supplement is made again only when a table or figure it shows, or a caption, changes. A caption is what the manuscript says where it names the file: a LaTeX figure's or table's `\caption`, or the line naming it in parentheses, such as `Table S4. Note types by species (S02_tableS4_note_types_by_species.csv)` or an item in a list. For a file found by its label, it's the line that starts with the label, as a caption or a list's item does, from the label on, so `- **Table S4.** Note types by species` gives `Table S4. Note types by species`, just as it would with the file named after it, and it takes its place in the document from that line; with no such line, it's the label itself. Else the heading is the label it goes by, with any caption after it; a file's name in a caption is left out. An SVG figure is shown in the supplement's PDF only when `rsvg-convert` is installed (`brew install librsvg`); else the PDF names it, and the Word file shows it.

**What's never lost.** Proxenos keeps, in the vault (`clones/folders/state.json`), what each file was made from and what it wrote. A note whose source changed is written again only while it's still exactly as Proxenos wrote it; one you edited or commented on stays as you left it, until you delete it. When the paper moves on in git, a copy of the manuscript you changed moves to `Earlier/<name> - manuscript <date>.docx` before the newer one is written, so your comments stay; so does a PDF you annotated, the supplement, or a file in `Supplement/` you changed. Proxenos reads nothing else in the folder and shares nothing.

## Folder settings

Three sources live in folders Proxenos can't guess, so each has a setting, in the page's Settings or in `.env`. A leading `~` is expanded.

| Setting | Points at | When it's not set |
|---|---|---|
| `PROXENOS_SPOTIFY_EXPORT` | The folder holding `Streaming_History_Audio_*.json` | `proxenos ingest spotify` stops and names the setting. `proxenos sync` skips Spotify. |
| `PROXENOS_WEBSITE_REPO` | Your site's local git repo | `proxenos ingest website` stops and names the setting. `proxenos sync` skips the website. |
| `PROXENOS_SLACKDUMP_DIR` | A folder holding `slackdump_*` runs | Only the repo's `data/` folder is searched. |

A path passed on the command line (`--export`, `--repo`, `--root`) always wins over the setting.

## What the data can and can't tell Proxenos

- **It records what you did and said, not why.** Your reasons, doubts and the things you thought but never wrote down only come from what you tell Proxenos directly and from your reactions on the page.
- **Most of it is who you have been.** A message from years ago is evidence about you then. Every record keeps its date, the self-model is always "as of" a date, and recent evidence counts for more. The upside is that Proxenos can model how you change, not just a snapshot.

## Where to start

Voice comes in registers (personal chat, work chat, email), and the self-model keeps them apart instead of averaging them. A good order:

1. **What you wrote:** your messages (WhatsApp, Slack), sent email, your website and notes. This gives voice and beliefs.
2. **What you read and watched:** YouTube, Chrome, notes, papers. This gives interests and taste.
3. **How you live:** Calendar, Strava, Spotify. This gives routine and rhythm.

## Privacy

1. **Raw data stays on your computer, outside this repo.** The repo is public code, so the vault lives elsewhere (picked on the setup page, or `PROXENOS_VAULT`; default `~/proxenos_vault`) and Proxenos refuses a vault inside the repo. Keep it out of folders a cloud service syncs (iCloud syncs Desktop and Documents by default), and turn on FileVault.
2. **Other people are in your data.** You can consent for yourself, not for everyone you've talked to. Proxenos models you: your side of conversations and how you relate to people. It doesn't build profiles of others, and their words are kept as context, not as subjects.
3. **Work and school accounts are institutional.** Mail and chat from them may hold information about colleagues, students, patients or research participants that isn't yours to share. Check your institution's policy on sending it to an external model, and leave those sources out if in doubt.
4. **Raw data never goes to the model wholesale.** Years of email is tens of millions of tokens, mostly newsletters and notifications. Observers read one month of one source at a time and only your own side (sent mail, your messages, your plays). Later steps read the distilled self-model, and some also send the recent records their job needs, picked out in code; [privacy.md](privacy.md) lists each one. The tasks clone, to find what is asked of you, sends recent conversations you're part of, and leaves newsletters, notifications and promotions out. The care clone, the most sensitive, sends only records about getting health care (visits, tests, referrals, bills, insurance, records, symptoms), picked out in code by keyword from the last 120 days, and your medical records from Apple Health or a portal: those from the last 120 days, and the conditions, medications and allergies they list as still active; people in chats become "contact 1", "contact 2", and phone, account and member numbers, email addresses and links are blanked before anything leaves. A result's value, unit and reference range are sent as they are: the blanking is for identifiers, not results.
5. **Secrets stay on your computer:** API keys, tokens and app passwords go to the macOS Keychain, one item each, or to `.env` (which git ignores) when you run from source with one. The page shows whether each is set, never what it is. Never put them, or any personal data, in an issue or a commit. The Google and Microsoft sign-ins only ever ask to read: nothing is sent, moved or deleted through them.
