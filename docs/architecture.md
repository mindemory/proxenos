# Architecture: one master, many clones

There is exactly **one model of you**, and many clones that use it. Clones are roles, not separate personalities. If each clone kept its own idea of who you are, they'd drift apart within weeks and none of them would be you.

```
                    ┌──────────────────────── master ────────────────────────┐
  sources ──ingest──▶ raw/ ──observers──▶ observations/ ──consolidate──▶ self/ │
                    └──────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┘
                                                   │ read-only
          ┌──────────┬──────────┬──────────┼──────────┬──────────┬──────────┐
       tracker     tasks     advisor   network    recall      work      web page
       (feed)   (on your   (advice)  (people,  (memories  (drafts,   shows outputs,
                  plate)              ideas)   for today) never sent) collects reactions
          └──────────────── outputs + your reactions ──────────────────────┘
                                                   │
                                     back to the master as evidence
```

## The pieces

- **Ingesters** (`proxenos ingest <source>`, or `proxenos import` for downloaded exports): pull each source into `raw/`. The supported sources are listed in [data_sources.md](data_sources.md).
- **Observer clones**, one per source: read new raw data and write dated notes on what it says about you (`observations/<source>/YYYY-MM.md`). The WhatsApp observer is the expert on how you talk to friends; the Spotify one on your moods and routines. They only ever read *your* side of conversations, and never a draft of Proxenos's you sent nearly unchanged, whose words are the model's (see work, below).
- **The master** (`proxenos master`):
  - runs everything on a schedule;
  - is the **only writer** of `self/` (identity, interests, taste, voice, routines, open questions);
  - folds new observations and your reactions into `self/`. Fast state (what you're into this week) updates directly; slow state (values, beliefs, voice) changes as proposals you approve.
  - Every change is a commit in the vault's private git history, so how you've changed is a `git log`.
- **Worker clones** (`proxenos clone <name>`), one per job. They read `self/`, do one job, and write output for you to react to. They never change `self/` directly.
  - **tracker:** a research and news feed, ranked against your interests.
  - **tasks:** what's on your plate, from recent conversations and what you told Proxenos on the home page (`proxenos.told`: a fact for now, or a rule every draft follows). The sync runs it too, at most hourly and only when something was said, so the list keeps up through the day. A task at a set time also gets when and where, and those become a calendar to subscribe to (`clones/tasks/plans.ics`, served at `/api/plans.ics`), and, with `PROXENOS_PLANS_CALENDAR` naming one of your calendars in the Mac's Calendar app, events on that one, which syncs them to its account (Google, say). Only the events it made are ever changed or deleted. A task can be done or called off: a plan called off (the visit cancelled, the ask withdrawn) leaves the calendar and its drafts fold away, as a done one's do. The same model call names the drafts waiting on you that the messages made moot (someone else did it, you said it another way, the plan changed), and a draft whose task is done goes too: each is marked overtaken, with why, and the page folds it away unless you say it's still needed.
  - **care:** where your health care stands, from the health-care admin in your mail, calendar, chats and notes: a timeline, open items and who they wait on, scripts for calls and messages, and a printable brief for the next clinician. It restates your records and never diagnoses.
  - **advisor:** small, evidence-based changes to how you work and live.
  - **choices:** once a week, what's worth taking on and what's worth letting go of, by your own goals. It reads the openings that reached you (calls for papers and abstracts, fellowships, grants, talks, programs, events), from mail, newsletters and mailing lists included, Slack channels for announcements, and the openings you kept from Ask (a job or call in a post you shared from your phone, read until its deadline passes, or for 90 days without one, and weighed first, as chosen by you, whatever its words), and what your time goes to: meetings and the hours they take, invitations not answered, chats waiting on you for over a week, old tasks, mail you never write back to, busy channels you never post in. Health-care mail is left to the care clone. One model call then picks up to five openings, each with why it fits you, cited from your records, its deadline and a first step (a draft, which the work clone writes that night, or an errand to start in one tap), including openings no one announced, such as writing your work is ready for; and up to five things to skip, each with its cost, its evidence and, when someone is owed an answer, a decline drafted. Every kept opening is among the week's to take on, with its deadline, as the model took it on or else as Ask showed it, and its first step is the draft Ask wrote. Each item takes useful, not me or doing on the page; what you mark not me or doing never comes back (a kept opening too), and the next week learns which kinds and senders you find useful.
  - **jobs:** every job lead in one place, each night. In code with no model it gathers the records that carry one: LinkedIn's mail (job alerts, recruiters' InMail and message notices, applications sent; any address at linkedin.com or a subdomain, as LinkedIn's help page names its senders), Y Combinator's Work at a Startup, which has no API, so the site's emails noticing each founder's message are the way in, with the link to answer it there, job boards' mail and any mail saying an application was received, recruiters writing to you directly (mail to you as a person with a recruiter's phrases: a role at, your background, open to, salary), LinkedIn chats with those phrases, list and Slack posts announcing positions, the jobs among the week's choices, and the jobs, postdocs and residencies in posts you shared to Ask, kept or not (fellowships, calls for papers and workshops stay with Choices). The model then reads the records not read yet, in batches that fit a local model, and lists the jobs in them: company, role, place, mode, pay, deadline, the link to apply on, and who's behind it on which channel. A job is its company and role, so an alert, a founder's message and a post you shared are one job with three sources, merged by id into `clones/jobs/jobs.json`, which also remembers the records read, so a re-run reads nothing twice. A second call per handful of jobs judges each from the self-model: apply, maybe or skip and why, the best way in (the apply link, a reply to the founder's message, a note to the poster, a referral, an email), which of your newest academic CV and industry résumé fits (found as errands finds a CV), and whom to contact: the poster or messager first, then the best one or two people you know there, from the network by org and your LinkedIn connections by company, with how you know them. The first step of each job to apply for or maybe is drafted in your voice into Drafts (`job-<id>`, following on from your earlier messages with that person when the vault has them), and where your CV goes too the job offers an errand that attaches it in Mail. You set each job's status on the page (new, interested, applied, interviewing, closed); applied is also suggested with no model, from mail you sent to the job's contact or company after it reached you with your CV or saying you apply, or a receipt of your application, for you to confirm. Nothing is sent or applied for.
  - **watch:** once a week, what to watch next. It reads every film you've logged on Letterboxd (an export, and the public diary feed that sync reads every run), with your ratings and hearts, your watchlist, and how you marked earlier picks. One model call then picks up to eight films and six shows you haven't seen, each with why it suits you and the films you loved that it follows from, and up to three films from your watchlist that suit this week. Code drops anything you've seen or marked seen or not for me, and links each pick to Letterboxd and to JustWatch, which says where it streams. Letterboxd keeps films, so the shows you mark on the page are what it learns your taste in TV from. What you mark want stays on the page until you mark it seen.
  - **network:** the people you know, how they connect, and project and outreach ideas. The graph keeps room for the circles of earlier years (an old class, batch or flat on WhatsApp you hardly write in any more) beside the people you're in touch with now, and names each cluster the way you'd say where you know them from ("PhD lab, Boston"), with one model call that reads your self-model and each cluster's groups, organizations and years. A name sticks while a cluster keeps half its people; rename one on the page and yours wins (`clones/network/clusters.json`).
  - **recall:** memories relevant to today (below).
  - **projects:** where each of your long-running projects stands (a paper, an analysis), from what your messages, mail and notes say about it and, for a project with its code's folder, what the code shows: the commits on every branch since the last run, the branch the checkout is on and the documents those commits changed, so a draft on another branch isn't missed. It writes a status, a dated log, the next steps, what only you can decide, and up to two drafts a night that move it on. The projects are your own list, `clones/projects/projects.json`, each in a group if you give it one ("PhD", "Postdoc"), and a project's `brief.md` beside its status, what's known around it and the question, is read with its notes.
  - **steps:** the next piece of a project's work, done. A project on the list with its code's folder (`"repo"`, a git repository on this computer) and its data folders (`"data"`) takes one step a night, once its last one is accepted or discarded, and one more whenever you press "Do a step now" or queue one on the page's Projects view. The step is the one you queued, else one picked from the project's status and the steps before: run an analysis, fix a script, make a figure, draft a section as markdown. It's done on a new branch, `proxenos/<project>-<when>`, in a git worktree of its own, so your checkout is never touched, and it comes back as a report: what it did, what it found (numbers and figures), the files it changed, what it couldn't do and what's next. Accept merges the branch into the branch your checkout is on; Discard deletes the branch and the worktree. Nothing is pushed and no one is contacted. The work itself is done by a runner (`proxenos/agent.py`): Claude Code, headless, where it's installed, inside its sandbox and never outside it, since Claude Code stops if the sandbox can't start (its commands write only in the worktree and reach no network; nothing it does reads the vault, your keys and other tools' credentials, your mail and messages or your browsers' profiles; at most 30 minutes, 100 turns and a spending budget, `PROXENOS_STEP_BUDGET`), on the account Claude Code is signed in to, never Proxenos's API key. Without Claude Code, or when it can't start, the local model takes the step, and it can only write: it reads a few of the project's files, a long LaTeX or Markdown document (a paper's `main.tex`) only by the sections the step needs, named by heading from the document's outline, and all of it within what a local model's context holds beside its answer (`llm.fit`). It writes a new or short file whole and changes a longer one by edits, each a stretch of text copied from the file and what replaces it, so its answer stays short; an edit whose text isn't in the file exactly once isn't made, a file it read only in part is never written whole, and the report lists what wasn't applied. It runs nothing, and its report says so. `PROXENOS_STEP_RUNNER` (`claude` or `local`) picks one. Each step is `clones/projects/<id>/steps/<when>.json`, with what it made beside it. A step you ask for can name the branch it starts from (`base` in `queue.json`): a manuscript's, which Accept then merges back into, or the branch of a step still waiting on you ("Revise this step"), which it builds on (`builds_on`), so Accept merges both into the branch that step came from and marks it accepted too.
  - **the manuscript** (`proxenos/manuscript.py`, not a clone; git only, no model, and not part of the night): the project's paper, as `"manuscript"` names it in `projects.json` or as the code's history shows it (the newest `paper.md`, `manuscript.md`, `main.tex`, or `.tex` under `paper/` or `manuscript/`, on any local branch). Its versions are the commits that changed it on its branch and on the branches of steps still waiting on you; two of them compare a paragraph at a time and then word by word, and a step's changes the same way, code line by line. Every version is read with `git cat-file` at its commit, never from your checkout. Asking for a change on the page queues a step from the manuscript's branch, carrying its path. Editing it there commits your text on a new branch, `proxenos/<project>-edit-<when>`, made from the manuscript's branch in a worktree that's removed straight after, and records it as a step waiting on you (`kind: "edit"`), which Accept merges and Discard deletes like any other. `proxenos project manuscript ID` prints it all as JSON for the page.
  - **its Google Doc** (`proxenos/paperdoc.py`, through `proxenos/gdocs.py`; no model, and not part of the night): Open in Google Docs uploads the manuscript at its branch's tip to the user's own Drive with the `GDOCS_<NAME>` sign-in, whose only scope is `drive.file`, as one multipart upload converted to a Doc (`text/markdown` for Markdown, `text/plain` for LaTeX and the rest), sets no sharing, and exports the new Doc straight back: that export is the baseline. The record (`doc_id`, link, path, branch, the commit uploaded, when) and the baseline are kept in `clones/projects/<id>/gdocs/`, private, and a project has one doc unless a fresh one is asked for. Bring back its changes exports it again; if it isn't the baseline, the text the baseline came from, the baseline and the new export are split into paragraphs as the diff splits them, and when the first two have as many, `difflib` matches the baseline's against the new export's, so paragraphs that stayed keep the manuscript's bytes and only those edited, added or deleted take the Doc's text (when the counts differ, the export is taken whole; `manuscript.rebuild`, which the folders clone shares, as it does `manuscript.pull_start`). That goes to the same edit as above, from the commit the baseline came from, so it waits on the user as a step and Accept merges it with whatever changed in git since; the new export becomes the baseline once the step exists, and the next pull waits until it's decided. The Doc's comments (`comments.list`) are read on each pull and on request and kept beside the record for the page, never sent to a model. `proxenos project gdocs ID [open|fresh|pull|comments]` prints it as JSON for the page.
  - **folders:** each project as a folder in a cloud drive the user already syncs on this computer (`PROXENOS_PROJECT_FOLDER`; unset, it does nothing), so they read, edit, comment on and share its manuscript and Proxenos's notes in Word or Google Docs. No model, and nothing shared: the drive uploads the folder, and who sees it is the user's choice. After the projects and steps each night, each project gets `<folder>/<group or Other>/<name>/`, names made safe for every OS (none of `/ \ : * ? " < > |`, no dot or space at either end, no name Windows reserves, at most 60 characters), of Word files made by pandoc (`PROXENOS_PANDOC`, else the PATH's; without it, nothing is done and it says to `brew install pandoc`): the manuscript at its branch's tip, written out of git with `git archive` (with the text, bibliography, style and image files beside it, PDF figures among them, but not its own PDF, up to 200 files and 100 MB) into a temporary folder and converted there, never from the checkout, Markdown as it is and LaTeX with `--citeproc` and the `.bib` files beside it, so one way; its PDF, made in that same checkout (LaTeX by `tectonic -X compile --untrusted`, `PROXENOS_TECTONIC` else the PATH's or Homebrew's; Markdown by pandoc with tectonic, else XeLaTeX, LuaLaTeX, pdfLaTeX or Typst), its source the Word file's with `pdf` added, so the two are made again together and only then, and a build that failed is kept as failed, with why, until that source changes; only with no engine is the PDF committed beside the manuscript copied, as About this folder says; the files the manuscript names copied into `Supplement/`, and, when it names a table or a figure, `Supplementary information.docx` and `.pdf` made from one Markdown file: each `.csv`, `.tsv` (and `.xlsx` with openpyxl) as a pipe table cut to 40 rows and 12 columns, and each figure, under the caption found where the manuscript names it, then the data files by name; and the project's `status.md`, `brief.md`, open drafts (`clones/drafts/project-<id>-*.md` with status draft or approved) and what `projects.json` says of it, and a note on the folder itself. A paused project gets one only when it has something to show. `clones/folders/state.json` keeps, per file, the SHA-256 of what it was made from (for the manuscript, its path and the blob ids of the files converted with it) and of the bytes written. A note whose source changed is written again only while its bytes are still those written; otherwise the user's copy stays and counts as kept. The manuscript is brought back first: when its bytes are neither those written nor those last brought back, a Markdown one is converted back to Markdown (`--standalone`, so its front matter comes back as front matter; pipe tables; citations unescaped) and rebuilt with `manuscript.rebuild` against `clones/folders/<id>/baseline.md`, the Markdown of the `.docx` as written, saved when it was. A text that differs from the manuscript at the commit the copy was made from goes to `manuscript.edit(..., source="folder")`, whose step and commit say it came from the Drive folder; the baseline and the bytes brought back are recorded once the step exists, and while it waits, newer changes wait in the copy (`manuscript.pull_start`). Then, when the source moved on, a copy the user changed is moved to `Earlier/<name> - manuscript <date>.docx` before the newer one is written, unless it holds changes still waiting to come back. Files are swapped in whole. `proxenos clone folders [--project ID] [--pull-only]` runs it by hand.
  - **journal:** the day, written down, so you never have to narrate it. Each night it writes the day that just ended (`clones/journal/<date>.json`; the page's Journal view writes today's on demand) from what the other clones and sources already hold: what got done (tasks closed, drafts sent, steps accepted, forms filled), where you wrote and what you told Proxenos, each project's commits that day, what's waiting on others, and what's next (tomorrow's calendar, tasks due soon or marked high, drafts to send, care's open items). One model call then writes a few sentences on how the day went and the three things to do next, from those lines alone; without a model the lines stand alone.
  - **life:** a diary of your life by month and day, years back, where the journal only goes forward. A day is a digest with no model, read from the memory index in local time: whom you wrote to and where, with counts and the opening of your longest message to each, and who wrote to you, by name (only your own words come out of the index; other people's stay in it), the day's events (from the calendar's own files, which know an all-day event), the titles of what you wrote down, your activity (walks, runs and rides and listening from Strava's and Spotify's month files, what you watched and searched on YouTube, photos' albums and who's tagged, films logged, sites browsed), health records dated that day by title, anything else counted by source, and the journal's brief where there is one. A month is one model call, held to a schema, and reads like a diary: an abstract of two to four sentences for the month's card; an essay in the first person, several paragraphs and about 600 to 1,200 words, through the month's themes and turning points; an entry in the first person for each day that mattered (about 6 to 15, at least one in most weeks with records: care and medical events, big conversations, decisions, trips, milestones, how you were doing), plain and in the past tense; and three to five highlights. The essay comes in the same call: the whole answer is a few thousand tokens, well inside the quarter of a local model's context it may answer in, and the prompt is trimmed to fit as before. It's written from what you said about the month yourself, first: what you marked not true on the page, with what's true in your words, which overrides everything else; your own abstract and essay, where you edited them, as the strongest guide to what's true; and what you told Proxenos that month (your facts, not your rules, nor the ones you removed), as its milestones, which must be in the abstract, the essay and the highlights. Then the month's health and care (care's timeline events dated in it, which are your own record and name people only by role, and your health records' titles), the observers' notes about you that month, and its days (the records' titles and the days inside an untrusted tag, since others may have named the chats and written the titles), trimmed to fit. A highlight in your own words is added for a milestone the model's highlights leave out (none shares two of its less common words), and anything it writes again word for word that you marked not true is dropped. Health and care are as central as they were, as the records state them: never diagnosed or speculated on, never played down. Walks, runs, rides and listening reach the model only as counts a week ("rode twice, hiked once"), and the Strava and Spotify observers' notes not at all, so no distance, pace or heart rate leads a month; a highlight or a sentence of an entry that is still mainly about a workout is dropped in code (a small pattern: a ride, a run or a hike with its distance, pace or heart rate), unless your own words that day (what you wrote to people or down, or told Proxenos) were about one. Entries on a day outside the month are dropped, and two on one day are one. No praise, no advice, feelings only as your own words show them, and no pronouns assumed. It's kept as `clones/life/months/YYYY-MM.json`. How many records each day holds is counted over the whole index in one query, kept in `clones/life/calendar.json`, and counted again only for the month files the index reads again, so after a sync it takes milliseconds. What you change on the page goes in two append-only files that only the page writes, private to you like the rest: `clones/life/edits.jsonl`, your own abstract and essay for a month (the latest line of a month counts), and `clones/life/corrections.jsonl`, what you marked not true (the essay by paragraph, a highlight or a day's entry) with what you said is true, keyed by month, item and the text marked, so a line sent twice changes nothing, and taken back with an undo line. An edit is shown in place of what was written until the month is written again. The night writes the month that just ended, rewrites the current one at most once a week, any month you told Proxenos something about (or removed it) or marked not true since it was written, the next night whatever the week says, then up to four older months with no diary, newest first (a month needs three days with records, or something you told Proxenos about it), so years fill in over a few weeks on a local model; a month whose diary failed waits a week. It never writes a month you edited since its last write: "Write again" on the page does, after asking. A month written before there was an essay (no `essay`), or before there were entries (a `story` and no `entries`), still shows, and counts as having no diary, so the night writes it again in the new form. `state/life.json` says what it wrote, how many months are left and which failed, and a night with nothing due asks no model. The page's Life view lists years and months, each with its abstract and highlights; opens a month's essay, which can be read aloud, then its diary, entry by entry, then its days, each under its entry with what the records hold from it; lets you edit the abstract and essay, mark a paragraph, a highlight or an entry not true with what's true in your words (struck out at once, and undone the same way), and writes a month on demand.
  - **rhythms:** your weeks over time, side by side, where life tells them as a diary. Each ISO week, in local time and with no model, it counts listening from Spotify's month files (hours, podcasts and audiobooks among them, plays of 30 seconds or more, distinct artists, the top five, and the mix of genres and moods by the hours of music behind them), movement from Strava's (rides with km, hours and climb; runs, hikes and walks with km and hours; anything else counted), messages from the memory index (sent and received per channel, among the sources whose messages are with people, so not chats with an AI assistant, and how many people and chats you wrote to; nobody's words leave the index), and events and their hours from the calendar's own files (one on two calendars counts once; not birthdays or what was called off). Each month file's share of each week is kept in `clones/rhythms/files.json`, with people and events as short hashes, and counted again only when the file changes or the time zone does; the series is `clones/rhythms/weeks.json`. Spotify's export has no genres, so the model labels each artist once from a fixed vocabulary (fifteen genres, eight moods), given the artists' names and nothing else, fifty to a call, the most played first, up to 300 a night; an answer outside the vocabulary is dropped, and an artist it can't place waits a month. Labels are kept in `clones/rhythms/artists.json`, and coverage is the share of music time whose artist has one; with no model the mixes say "unlabelled" and the rest is counted all the same. What stands out (the period with the most listening, the longest run of weeks with a ride, the busiest week of messages, how the top mood moved) is written in code for each range and resolution, and says what happened, never why. The page's Rhythms view groups the weeks a week, two weeks or a month at a time, as the clone does, over three months, a year or everything, with small aligned charts, each period's top genre, mood and artist, and a table.
    - **Month cards** (`clones/portrait.py`, with `proxenos.mood` and `proxenos.musicbrainz`): a card for each local month, like a library card, on how it felt beside what you listened to and wrote. *Mood* comes only from your own writing: the messages you sent on the channels with people, read from the memory index's rows marked as yours, and the new part (`body_new`) of the mail you sent, never what you quoted or forwarded; other people's messages aren't read for it. Each sentence gets VADER's compound score (MIT, made for short social-media text, emoji and slang among them) on this computer with no model, and a day's valence is the mean of those with a feeling once there are three. A model on this computer, and only one (`ollama:` or `local:`), names each day's feelings from a fixed list of twelve, a month's days in one call with each day's most charged sentences; with a hosted model that step is skipped, so your words never leave the computer. The NRC Emotion Lexicon was weighed and left out, since its terms bar commercial use without a licence. The days roll up into the month's top feelings (or, without them, its brighter, mixed and heavier days), its arc (each week's valence, the brightest and the hardest) and how much it varied, beside your usual spread. *Music* is each day's hours from Spotify's month files and the month's genres and moods from the artists' labels, with *kinds of music* from MusicBrainz: Spotify's export has no genres, its audio features and recommendations closed to new apps in November 2024, and though `GET /artists/{id}` still returns genres, the export has no artist ids and development-mode apps lost the batch lookups in February 2026. The night looks up the 120 most played artists not yet asked, by name alone, one request a second, keeps the answers in `clones/rhythms/musicbrainz.json` (an artist with none is asked again after 90 days) and stops at the first error; `PROXENOS_MUSICBRAINZ=off` stops it. MusicBrainz's tags and genres are CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, read for your own use and never shipped. *Writing* is your words by channel and in notes, with documents you changed counted apart, beside your usual month (the median of the twelve before that hold any, cached in `clones/rhythms/words.json`, and pro rata while a month is going). *Relations* set each day's valence beside its music (hours, and the shares of upbeat and of melancholic music) as Pearson's r with the days it rests on and a two-sided p from Fisher's z, on the same day and a day apart (music then mood, mood then music), and the days with and without each of the month's top two genres, kinds and artists; a relation is notable from |r| of 0.3 with ten days, or four days each side, and each is said in a sentence that never says which led and adds "it could be chance" where p is 0.05 or more. The *story* is a short paragraph in the second person by the local model from the card's numbers and names only, never anyone's words, else written in code from them. A card (`clones/rhythms/months/YYYY-MM.json`) holds numbers, labels and artists' names, each day's words only as a hash, and the time and size of every file it read: the night makes one again only when those change, when it was made before its month ended, when its music was under 95% labelled and the labels moved, or when it waits on a model that's now on this computer, and asks the model again only for days whose words changed and a story whose numbers did. It makes this month's and last month's, then up to six older months a night, newest first; `proxenos clone rhythms --month YYYY-MM` makes one now. The view lists the cards newest first beside one opened large: its story, mood, music and writing, the days as aligned sliders (mood above or below zero, hours of music) that say which day they're on, a table of the days, and the relations, every one tried behind a disclosure.
  - **work:** drafts of replies, emails, plans and reading notes in your voice, and the first steps and declines of the week's choices. Drafts only; nothing is sent. Each direct chat where someone else had the last word in the past week gets a reply (WhatsApp, Messenger, Instagram, LinkedIn, email, and Slack direct messages and threads that mention you or that you're in), drafted from the conversation, your own last messages in that chat (how you write to that person), and the pages linked in what's waiting, fetched and read first. A chat that ends on thanks or a notice needs no reply and is filed away; one that asks you something, or proposes a time, always gets one, whatever the model says. With an API key, `proxenos sync` drafts these replies as chats come in: only chats with no draft yet, at most five a sync, so a quiet sync makes no model call. Every draft also gets the drafts you changed before sending, beside what you sent, and ahead of them the writing rules you kept (`self/voice_rules.md`). A rewrite Think made of a draft at your asking is logged as an edit marked `via: think`, so no run writes over it, but the model wrote it, so it's never learned from as your change; your own edits to it after are. The night's last step learns those rules the [PROSE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.23815) way (`proxenos.voice.lessons`): once five or more changed drafts are new, one model call per batch of them infers short rules ("To Mom, in Marathi-English mix, no sign-off"), and one per batch of rules checks each against your own sent messages outside those drafts, keeping only those that hold on at least three. A rule about health, or quoting more than a few words, is never kept. The rules kept are a proposal (`self/proposals/voice_rules.md`), like the self-model's slow files, and reach a draft only once you accept them with `proxenos master accept`. A draft you copy and send nearly unchanged (closeness 0.9 or more, `fidelity.NEARLY`) comes back through the sources as your own message, but its words are the model's: each sync's fidelity measure lists the messages it was sent as in `clones/drafts/drafted.json`, and no example of your writing, check of a rule, voice-model answer, held-out reply or observer note reads them as yours. A draft you changed more stays your writing, and is learned from as a change.
- **Calls** (`proxenos call start NAME`, or the page's Calls view): a phone call the user starts from a call sheet, through their own Twilio account. It waits on hold saying nothing, tells a person picking up from the hold's recordings, says it's an automated assistant calling for the user, and rings the user, who presses 1 to be put through; or it asks for what the sheet lists itself, a model turn at a time, with code that replaces any reply agreeing to pay and puts the user through for anything it doesn't have. The transcript and what came of each ask stay in the vault ([calls.md](calls.md)).
- **Forms** (`proxenos form PATH`): a PDF form filled from your records. It reads the form's own fields, or, on a flat PDF, its labels, the lines and boxes beside them and its circles and checkboxes; searches memory for each label; and asks the model to fill each blank from what it found, citing where each answer came from. Code then keeps only answers the records it was shown actually contain, and never sends or fills a line for an SSN or another ID, an account or card number, or a signature. The filled copy lands beside the form as `-filled.pdf`, with what's left for you listed.
- **Preprints** (`proxenos preprint refs|bundle`, `proxenos/preprint.py`; no model, not part of the night): a LaTeX paper made ready for arXiv. `refs` reads the paper's BibTeX with a small parser of its own (macros, nesting, `@comment` skipped) and looks each entry up by DOI at Crossref (DataCite for a DOI Crossref doesn't hold), by arXiv id at arXiv's API, else by title at Crossref then OpenAlex, one host a second apart (arXiv three), through links.py so only on the public internet, naming Proxenos with no email address. Titles compare by their words with LaTeX, accents and case aside; a nearly matching title counts only when the first author agrees, so a review named after a paper isn't taken for it. A web entry is fetched and its page title set beside the entry's. What the indexes found is kept in `state/preprint.json` for 30 days. `bundle` follows `\input` and `\include` from the main file, resolves figures as pdflatex would, adds the local classes and styles and the `.bbl` (refusing a missing or stale one), takes the comments out of the TeX, and writes the `.tar.gz` with no user name in it, warning of home paths, stray email addresses and TODOs by the line in your own file.
- **Memory** (`proxenos index`, no model): a full-text index (SQLite FTS5, `vault/memory/index.sqlite`) over every raw record, searchable by words, person, source and date, with the surrounding conversation for any hit. It is derived data, rebuilt from `raw/` at any time, and `proxenos sync` keeps it current. Beside it, a vector index (`vault/memory/vectors.sqlite`, `proxenos index --vectors`) holds the last year by meaning: a chat's day of messages, or one email, note or document, embedded by a small model on this computer (Ollama, `qwen3-embedding:0.6b`, even when the model that writes is on another server, as Ollama alone takes a small context per request and so keeps the two in memory together), so a question finds what answers it even when the words differ. Each sync embeds what's new for a few minutes; reply research searches it first. The **recall** clone (`proxenos clone recall`) turns it into proactive memory: for today's calendar, open tasks, feed items and closest people it finds the past conversations, mail and notes that bear on them, or nothing when nothing fits.
- **Ask** (`proxenos ask`, the page's Ask view): a question, a pasted message or up to eight screenshots, picked on the page or shared to the installed page from a phone's share sheet. The model reads the screenshots (who wrote what), looks through memory as a reply does (the person, and what the answer depends on, by meaning and by words), then answers from the self-model and the "right now" note, and drafts a reply in your voice when it's a message to you. A local model reads two or more screenshots one at a time first, so each call holds one picture. When what's shared shows openings (job posts, postdoc and residency calls, fellowships, scholar programs, calls for papers or abstracts, workshops, as LinkedIn and X are full of), the answer also lists them, apply first: what and who, where, on site or remote, the pay, the deadline and the link when shown, a verdict (apply, maybe or skip) and why, from the self-model and memory, and, for apply and maybe, a first step drafted in your voice (a note to whoever posted it when the post invites one, else a cover letter's opening paragraph, or a plan for a call for papers). Nothing is applied for, sent or submitted. Each ask is a file in `clones/ask/`; its drafts also land in Drafts. Keep on an opening (`proxenos ask --keep ASK OPENING`) adds it to the openings the week's choices weigh, in `clones/choices/kept.jsonl`, once however often it's kept.
- **Think** (`proxenos think`, the page's Think view): thinking anything through with Proxenos, over as many turns as it takes, where Ask answers once. A thread is about whatever the user brings (a decision, a message to send for themselves or someone else, a project, a day, a thought), and can start from a draft ("Think it through" on Drafts), a project (on Projects), a day (on Life) or a person; what it started from is read from the vault and kept at the thread's top. Each answer is one model call held to a schema, after research's fused search for the thread's topic (what was just said, the title, what it started from) and its gate, which leaves out the near misses. It reads the self-model, goals and values first, then open questions, interests, routines and taste (not voice), trimmed to fit; what the user told Proxenos; the journal's last three briefs; the records kept; what the thread started from; and its last twelve messages. Memory's records, the journal and what a thread started from go inside an untrusted tag; the user's own words, the self-model and their notes don't. It answers in Proxenos's own plain voice, a few short paragraphs, then what matters here (each line citing the part of the self-model, the note, the record or the day it comes from, a line citing anything else is dropped in code, and a record opens in Memory), questions to sit with or to ask someone, and up to three optional next steps; a step that's a message has "Draft this", which puts it in Ask's box to draft in the user's voice. It lays out considerations and trade-offs and never decides for the user, says when the records say nothing, diagnoses nothing, and for health, law, money or immigration names the professional to ask. Each thread is a file in `clones/think/`, written with the user's message first and then the answer, which the page follows by the job, not the file, so polling runs no command; without a model set up nothing is written and it says how to set one up.
- **Errands** (`proxenos clone errands "GOAL"`, the page's Errands view): something to get done, such as reaching someone about a role, with your CV. An errand reads the goal, looks the person up, finds a way to reach them from your own mail and chats (an address they wrote from, one you wrote to, a chat with them through Beeper; never a guessed address), or, with no address there, from their organization's own web pages (`proxenos.lookup`: a search for their name and organization, then only that organization's pages among the results, with every page the address is on), finds your newest CV, reads the emails you wrote before for the same kind of reach-out, and writes the message, plus, when there's no way to reach them, a note asking someone you know there for an introduction. Then it stops one tap short: the page shows each message, editable, with every step the errand took and why, and Send sends that one message, by Mail with the CV attached or through Beeper. Each night, errands still waiting on an address look again. Each is a file in `clones/errands/`.
- **Offers** (`proxenos clone offers "GOAL"`, the page's Offers view; not part of the night): what several people or firms offered for one goal, side by side, with a reply to each. It searches memory for the goal over the last N months (12 by default), by its words, any of its telling words and its meaning, as Think does, and reads each record found with the rest of its conversation (`memory.context`). It groups them by counterpart: a firm by its mail's registered domain, someone writing from a personal address (gmail.com and the like) by that address, a person in a chat by the chat. Only others' messages make a counterpart, never bulk or automated mail or the user's own organization; the user's own messages to them come along as what was asked. Up to twelve, with their newest eight messages each, are read one model call apiece, their messages inside an untrusted tag: who they are, the address they write from, the first step and its price, the whole and its price, the other prices, what's credited, the terms and what's missing. Each figure the model gives is held to their own messages in code, and one that isn't there is dropped and named as unconfirmed; an address stands only when their records have it; the last contact and who owes whom a reply are counted in code. One more call writes what to compare them on from the rows alone, its sentences with a figure the rows don't have left out. It writes `clones/offers/<slug>.json` and `.md` (and, with `--project`, a section of that project's `brief.md`, replaced on each run). Then a reply to each, as the user (`proxenos.voice`, `proxenos.told`): it asks for what's missing and may say someone else offers a free or cheaper first step or quoted less, never who, and a figure only when the user's own messages to them already gave it. Each reply is an errand in `clones/errands/`, ready to send to the address in the records or in a chat Beeper can send in, or to one on their own pages (`proxenos.lookup`) when lookups are on, or else waiting for one; a run again rewrites those not yet sent.
- **Compare** (`proxenos compare`, the "Which writes like you?" card in Settings, under the model): which of two models writes replies most like you, judged blind by you, where `proxenos voice eval` scores them by machine. A round takes your replies in your ten most recent conversations, one each, where a draft would have been written (someone else had the last word, saying something to answer, and you'd written there before) and the reply isn't a draft of Proxenos's you sent nearly unchanged, and has both models draft each again through the work clone's own reply code, as of the moment before you answered: the chat as `chats.waiting` would have given it then, your own writing from before it only (`voice.Writing.until`, which also leaves out a draft you changed and sent after it), and your notes, "right now" note, calendar and sent posts as they stood then. No model sees the reply, anything after it in that chat, or anything you wrote after it. What research finds in memory and the linked pages are left out, since memory holds the reply itself and everything since; both models get the same prompt, so the only difference is the model. A drafting model is pointed at with `llm.using`, which also logs its calls as `compare`; a voice model writes as it does for a reply. One model drafts all its replies before the other, so two local models aren't swapped in and out of memory each time. The page shows the conversation and the two drafts as A and B, in an order drawn for each reply; you pick A, B, both fine or neither, and only then see which model wrote which, how close each came to what you sent (fidelity's measure) and, if you open it, what you sent. Results per model: the share of picks won, with a Wilson 95% interval, and the drafts' mean closeness; under about 20 picks the page says it tells little. A new round of the same two models takes other replies, so rounds add up. Each round is `state/compare/<id>.json`, written after every draft so the page shows how far it got, with its picks appended to `<id>.picks.jsonl`, the latest for a reply counting, so a pick can be changed and never races a round still drafting. Nothing is sent to anyone.
- **Privacy** (`proxenos privacy`, the page's Privacy view): every model call leaves one line in `state/model-calls.jsonl` (when, which part asked, the model, where it ran, characters and tokens in and out, how many numbers and secrets `proxenos.redact` blanked before it went to Anthropic, never the words), and this adds up the last 30 days by where they ran and by part, beside what stays on the computer (the vault by folder, whether a cloud syncs it, FileVault), what's read from elsewhere, and whether notices go to the phone, through which server (never the topic) and what one carries. Other people's words (their messages, the pages they link to, the records memory finds) go into the tasks, drafts, care, ask and think prompts inside a tag drawn fresh for each prompt (`proxenos.untrusted`), which the model is told holds data, never instructions.
- **Notices** (`proxenos/notify.py`, no model): one short push to the user's phone through [ntfy](https://ntfy.sh), an open-source push service, when something new waits on them, so they hear of it without opening the page. Off unless `NTFY_TOPIC` is set; the topic is kept as a secret like a key (on ntfy.sh anyone who knows it can read it), and so is `NTFY_TOKEN`, for a server with access control. `PROXENOS_NTFY_SERVER` names the user's own server (default `https://ntfy.sh`). It's the last step of `proxenos sync` and `proxenos night`: it counts what the page's Home asks the user about, as Home counts it (drafts to send, care items that are their move, project steps waiting for review, open tasks overdue or due within three days), and sends at most one notice a run, only when a count went up since the last one. `state/notified.json` (0600) keeps the counts as of that notice, lowered as items are dealt with, so the next new one is news again. During quiet hours (`PROXENOS_QUIET_HOURS`, default `22:00-08:00` local time, or `off`) the notice waits and goes out on the first run after them, if what was new still waits. A notice says counts and kinds only ("2 drafts to send and 1 care item wait on you."), never a name, a message's words, a subject or a health detail, since the server is a third party and a notice shows on a lock screen. A failure is logged and tried again on the next run, and never fails the sync or the night. `proxenos notify test` (Settings' Send a test) sends a test notice. Nothing goes out from a demo vault.
- **Desktop app** (`desktop/`): the coming one download, unsigned for now. An Electron shell runs the page as a standalone Next.js server and `python -m proxenos schedule` with a Python of its own (python-build-standalone), with a menu-bar menu; `desktop/build.sh` builds a Mac `.dmg` or a Windows installer, and `.github/workflows/desktop.yml` builds both on GitHub by hand or for a `v*` tag ([apps.md](apps.md)).
- **Web page** (`web/`): local only. A landing page (`/`) with live counts from the vault, then the app (`/app`): a daily briefing, clone outputs, memory search, the current self-model, a Connect view (every source and whether it's connected, with buttons to sign in to Gmail and read WhatsApp) and sync status. Your reactions there are Proxenos's best training signal.

## Ground rules

- **Read and draft, never act.** No clone sends a message, buys anything, or posts as you; an errand's message goes only when you press Send on it. A project's step changes code only on a branch of its own, and it reaches your checkout only when you press Accept. Project folders are written only to the folder you chose, which your drive uploads, and nothing is shared for you. Acting on your behalf comes later, per clone, only after its predictions about you are measurably good, and always with your approval.
- **Only your behavior is evidence about you.** Clone outputs are tagged as made by Proxenos and never fed back as if you wrote them.
- **The model is yours to pick** (`proxenos/llm.py`): an open model on this computer, where nothing leaves it, or the Anthropic API with your own key, kept separate from any work or school account. A local model runs through Ollama (`ollama:<name>`) or any other app that serves OpenAI's API (`local:<name>`: LM Studio, llama.cpp's `llama-server`, Jan, LocalAI, vLLM) at `PROXENOS_LOCAL_URL`, which has to be this computer's own address (`localhost`, `127.0.0.0/8`, `::1`) and is reached through no proxy, following no redirect. Both take the schema as they write (Ollama's `format`, the server's `response_format`), fall back to the check alone when they can't, and log their calls as this computer's. The setup page lists their models with which fit this computer's memory (`proxenos/machine.py`: a model fits when its size plus 4 GB for its context is under 70% of the memory). Clones send the distilled self-model and public items. Observers send your own messages, never other people's. A project's steps go to Claude Code on its own account (or the local model): the project's name, its status and the steps before, and the code and data it reads.
